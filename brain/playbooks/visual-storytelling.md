# Visual storytelling and personal-theme playbook

Owner-approved working guidance distilled from user-supplied portfolio/design videos on 2026-09-29. Source IDs and time-based observations live in the external portfolio's `docs/VIDEO_RESEARCH_2026-09-29_ADDENDUM.md`; original media stays on the owner's device. These are design mechanisms, not performance evidence or permission to copy assets.

## Use when

- A portfolio should present a person and their work as a memorable story.
- The owner asks for a visual motif rooted in a real hobby, craft, identity or journey.
- Project films or scroll storytelling are being considered.
- Any new website is being designed: begin with a bespoke experience thesis and motion idea, even when the visual style is quiet or minimal.

## Method

1. Ask the brief for a real owner-supplied motif and why it matters. Examples: skating, music, field research. Do not infer an identity from visual taste or web history.
2. Map the motif to the site's information architecture: introduction → challenge/context → project decision → build/process → verified result → next chapter/contact.
3. Make each project a distinct episode: **situation → user/problem → system decision → real product action → observed/verified outcome → limits/source**. Keep the case page complete without its film.
4. Use one motif to aid orientation or explain motion (e.g. a skate-line connects chapters or wheel marks pace a scroll sequence). No long scroll lock, forced horizontal scrolling or animation-only content. Normal document scrolling remains the default.
5. Present 2–3 genuinely different visual concepts as standalone reviewable HTML plus a concise Markdown decision sheet. Recommend one, explain tradeoffs, and bind G2 approval to the exact files' hashes.
6. Motion teaches or signals a real state. Keep essential copy static and immediate. Offer keyboard/touch behavior and a reduced-motion/static equivalent. Test phone layouts and actual performance before adding 3D or heavy media.
7. A `Play film` action appears only when a real reviewed project film exists. Use user-approved footage/source material; click-to-play, no autoplay audio, native controls, captions, transcript, poster, source notes and a text equivalent. A `/brag` clip is promotional media, not proof of benchmark claims.
8. State the experience thesis in one sentence and storyboard at least three beats: first impression, a product/company-specific demonstration or story turn, and the next action. Make it visible in paired offline HTML/Markdown before production UI code. Offer distinct concepts so the owner can choose; quiet art direction still needs a distinctive interaction or motion language.

## Source lessons (video research; paraphrased)

- Story-led scroll sequences can make progression feel intentional when each scene advances the explanation; preserve the reader's control and ordinary vertical scrolling.
- A first scene can demonstrate what a product does before a visitor reads details; state the actual role/problem in text as well.
- Small hidden details may reward exploration, but never make navigation, evidence or contact discoverable only through an easter egg.
- A portfolio can derive memorability from a distinct visual language, a character/illustration and a project gallery. Use original imagery tied to the owner's story; don't copy the sampled site.
- 2D animation, cursor-aware objects and 3D scenes are optional craft choices. Add them after the content works, and only when input, reduced motion, mobile, accessibility, rights and LCP budgets are addressed.
- Marketing advice in a clip (SEO tools/checklists/ranking claims) is a research lead. Verify provider behavior and claims against current primary documentation; no checklist guarantees rank.

## Evidence and memory boundaries

- Store project-specific source IDs, timecodes, rough transcripts and claims in that project's `docs/TASTE_REPORT.md` / `docs/VIDEO_RESEARCH_*.md`.
- Store reusable paraphrased methods here; store explicit owner taste in `brain/preferences.md`; store tested mechanisms as provenance-linked entries in `brain/patterns/`.
- Build lineage belongs in `brain/builds/<slug>.json`: mark which motif, story beat, asset source and pattern produced each section. Store no raw client text, secrets, video binaries or private footage in the harness.
- `/learn` records owner feedback and outcomes after a project. Do not promote an untested animation pattern to TRUSTED solely because a video recommends it.
